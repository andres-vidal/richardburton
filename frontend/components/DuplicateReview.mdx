# Duplicate review

Steps through the records that look like one publication entered more than once
— the ones the composite key cannot catch, because a typo, a dropped accent or a
translator's initials make them technically distinct.

The queue on the left is one question per cluster, likeliest first; the pane on
the right is the evidence, side by side, because that is all there is to go on.

<Canvas of={DuplicateReviewStories.Default} />

## The three answers

- **Merge into the selected one** — they are one publication. The record chosen
  to keep survives and gains what the others hold; the rest leave the database.
  See [publication merge](?path=/docs/publications-publication-merge--docs) for
  what a merge does, and how taking it apart puts the records back.
- **Not duplicates** — they are different publications. The answer is
  remembered, so the pair is not offered again; without that the tail never
  clears. It can be taken back — see below.
- **Skip** — not now. Nothing is recorded and the cluster comes back.

<Canvas of={DuplicateReviewStories.KeepingTheOther} />

## Taking an answer back

Both lasting answers are reversible, which is what lets a reviewer work quickly
without the fear of being wrong once and forever.

- A **merge** is undone from the publication's history, which restores the
  records it absorbed. Merging also forgets any earlier "not duplicates" about
  the same records: it answers the same question, and later, so the stale
  answer would otherwise keep the pair hidden after the un-merge.
- **Ruled apart** sits under the queue in the same rail. Opening one shows the
  pair in the pane where a question would be — the same evidence, with nothing
  left to choose — and offers it back among the questions. It is part of the
  review rather than beside it: a reviewer who misread a cluster finds it where
  they answered it. The section is absent when nothing has been ruled apart.

<Canvas of={DuplicateReviewStories.RuledApart} />

## Usage

`DuplicateReview` reads nothing: the page reads the clusters on the server and
hands them over, and the exported `DuplicateReviewView` is the presentational
shell the stories drive.

Which record survives is the reviewer's choice. It opens on the one entered
first, since the others came along after it — a proposal, not a judgement about
which is right.
